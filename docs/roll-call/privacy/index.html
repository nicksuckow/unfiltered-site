Roll Call privacy policy

Last updated: November 15, 2026

Roll Call is an iPhone app by Nicholas Suckow. It shows you a few contacts each day to keep or set aside, and finds duplicates to merge. This page explains what Roll Call does with your information. For help using the app, see the support page.

What Roll Call collects

Roll Call doesn't collect any data. It has no accounts, no server, no analytics, no crash-reporting software, no ads and no in-app purchases. It makes no network requests. Its only links open the privacy policy and support pages in Safari.

In the App Store, Roll Call's privacy label says Data Not Collected. Roll Call sends your contacts nowhere, and nothing leaves your iPhone unless you export it.

Third parties

Roll Call has no third-party code that collects data, and it shares or sells nothing, because it has nothing to share. If you have turned on Share With App Developers in your iPhone's analytics settings, Apple may send the developer crash reports. Apple controls those reports, and they hold no contacts.

Why Roll Call needs your contacts

Roll Call asks for access to your contacts when you tap Continue during setup. It needs your address book to show you a few people each day to keep or set aside, and to find duplicates that can be merged. All of this happens on your iPhone. If you allow full access, Roll Call can look at your whole address book, which it needs to find duplicates across it.

If you share only some contacts

On iOS 18 and later you can share only some of your contacts. Roll Call then works on only the contacts you share, and a banner tells you it can only see those people. You can choose Share more or Open Settings at any time. Roll Call doesn't block you or nag you about it, and it never treats a contact it can no longer see as deleted.

Changes, and the copies Roll Call keeps

Nothing in your address book changes until you confirm it in Review. Before Roll Call removes or merges anything, it saves its own full copy of what it is about to change, including photos. It reads the copy back to check it, and if saving the copy fails, nothing is applied.

These copies appear in Recently removed, where you can restore them for 30 days. Once a copy is 30 days old, Roll Call deletes it the next time you open the app. The copies stay on your iPhone, in Roll Call's private storage. They are excluded from iCloud Backup, and they are deleted when you delete the app. Apart from these copies, Roll Call writes contact photos nowhere: the pictures on cards are kept in memory only, with no image cache.

Changes you confirm are made in your iPhone's address book, so iOS syncs them to the accounts your contacts belong to, such as iCloud or Google, just as it does for edits in the Contacts app.

From Recently removed you can also export a contacts file (.vcf) through the share sheet. Roll Call itself sends your contact data nowhere; exporting is the one way it leaves through Roll Call, and only because you chose it. The exported file is temporary and is deleted when the share sheet closes. Once you share it, though, it is in the hands of whatever app or person you shared it with.

What Roll Call stores on your iPhone

Roll Call stores only its settings, your progress as opaque record ids and one-way hashes (no names, phone numbers or email addresses), and the 30-day copies in Recently removed. Settings and progress are included in your iPhone's backups, like other app data. The copies in Recently removed are excluded from iCloud Backup, so a phone restored from a backup starts with an empty Recently removed.

Contact notes

Roll Call doesn't have Apple's permission to read contact notes, so it can't see them or back them up. Roll Call can't read contact notes yet, so notes on removed or merged-away cards won't be in Recently removed. Review shows you this line too. Removing or merging away a card can lose its note.

Reminders

Reminders are optional and off by default. If you turn them on, Roll Call schedules local notifications on your iPhone. Nothing is sent from a server, and Roll Call uses no push notifications. You can change the time or turn reminders off in Settings.

Turning off access and deleting your data

You can turn off Roll Call's access to your contacts at any time in Settings > Privacy & Security > Contacts. Doing so doesn't delete the copies in Recently removed.

To remove the copies, choose Delete these copies now in Recently removed, which deletes every saved copy at once. You can also delete the app, which removes them too.
